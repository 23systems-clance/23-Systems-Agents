Ray from MSP Sales Partners breaks down why most MSP SDR programs fail and what the top performers are doing differently. Drawing from managing 65+ SDRs and hiring over 100 for MSPs, he identifies five critical mistakes that kill SDR ROI (bad hires, dirty lists, weak offers, outdated scripts, and no sales infrastructure), then presents three advanced case studies from top-performing clients: the "Prospecting Crockpot" (disposition-based nurture sequences driving 15-20 appointments/month), the "OSR Light" (grooming SDRs into full salespeople), and "Mowing the Grass 2.0" (amplifying standard campaigns from 1 week/2 channels to 30 days/5 channels for 2-4x results).

1

The difference between a good and great SDR is 2-5x, not 10-20%

Hiring the cheapest person who can read a script will never produce results. The SDR role requires coachability, communication skills, resilience, and the ability to think on their feet. Being selective with hiring—even for an entry-level role—is the single most impactful lever for SDR performance.

"The difference between somebody good and somebody great is not 10 or 20%. It is two to 5x, and in your world when you're thinking of two to 5x the number of appointments per month, that is a lot of money."

Vendor lists from ZoomInfo, Apollo, etc. market "99% accuracy" but that's across all data points on a record. If the contact name and phone number are wrong, that record is 0% useful for SDR outreach. Either enrich/clean lists before handing them off, or accept 30-40% lower first-campaign returns while the SDR cleans as they go.

"If what's wrong in that record is the contact name and the phone number of that contact, then that record is 0% useful for the SDR unless they go research and clean it."

SDRs are selling time, not products—so give them a valuable offer

The prospect's price is their calendar time, which is often harder to get than their credit card. "Will you meet with my CEO for 15 minutes?" is a weak pitch. A specific, valuable offer (toolkit, assessment, case study walkthrough) dramatically increases both booking rates and show rates.

The Three-Tier Framework

5:12 — 8:48

Ray frames the SDR performance spectrum into three tiers based on two variables: foundation quality and strategic deployment.

  • Tier 1: Zero returns, goose eggs — caused by foundational mistakes
  • Tier 2: Good returns (3-5 appointments/month, positive ROI) — achieved by simply avoiding Tier 1 mistakes
  • Tier 3: Crushing it (10-20+ appointments/month) — doing the basics plus creative, strategic SDR deployment

Mistake #1: Hiring Anybody with a Pulse

9:16 — 14:26

The mindset of "find the cheapest person to read a script" is the most destructive mistake. Even as an entry-level role, SDRs need specific traits that dramatically separate performers from non-performers.

  • If you don't trust an SDR to go off-script, you might as well use an AI bot—and those don't work well yet either
  • Key traits: coachable, good communicator, resilient (handles rejection all day), willing to put in the work
  • Like hiring a Level 1 tech—the job is teachable, but you still need the right raw material
  • The fix: identify what it actually takes to be good at the role, then be selective even for entry-level

Mistake #2: Bad or No List Data

14:26 — 18:46

The most dangerous scenario is thinking you have clean data when you don't. Vendor "99% accuracy" claims are misleading because they measure across all data fields, not the ones that matter for outreach.

  • Raw vendor lists (ZoomInfo, Apollo, Seamless) typically have 30-40% breakage on contact-level data
  • You can either clean the list before handing to the SDR, or accept lower first-round results while they clean as they go
  • Either way, you must set expectations accordingly—a dirty list means discounted performance, not an SDR problem

Mistakes #3-5: Weak Offers, Outdated Scripts, No Sales Infrastructure

18:46 — 32:32

Three more foundational mistakes that compound to produce zero returns. Each one independently can cripple SDR performance.

  • Weak offer: "Will you meet with my CEO?" has no value proposition. Instead, offer something tangible (toolkit, assessment, case study) that justifies the prospect's time investment
  • Outdated scripts: The 3-minute monologue is dead. Permission-based openers, shorter scripts, and human-sounding delivery are what's working now. AI gatekeepers and phone screeners require tactical adaptation
  • No infrastructure: SDRs need regular sales meetings, call reviews, feedback, peer exposure, dashboards, and accountability. Without it, even good SDRs will see performance decline over time
  • LinkedIn voice notes converting well right now because they're demonstrably human

Case Study 1: The Prospecting Crockpot (15-20 Appointments/Month)

34:59 — 46:10

A client consistently producing 15-20 qualified appointments per month—and replicated the results with a second SDR, proving it's the system, not the person. The key insight: they think about ROI on a 6-18 month timeline, not per campaign.

  • Step 1: Run normal outbound campaigns (95-97% don't book—that's expected)
  • Step 2: Categorize every non-booking into 5-7 dispositions in the CRM (already have an MSP, in contract, bad timing, etc.)
  • Step 3: Each disposition triggers a tailored 3-6 month email sequence (weekly emails, 24 total, written thoughtfully—not AI-generated)
  • Step 4: Emails subtly create doubt and maintain top-of-mind awareness, customized to the specific objection
  • Step 5: Sequence completion triggers a callback task. SDR now calls with clean data, warm awareness, email engagement intel, and a strategic reason to reconnect
  • 70% of their results come from this process, according to the client
  • Microwave mentality (hot pocket in 90 seconds) vs. crockpot mentality (simmer for hours, dramatically better outcome)

Case Study 2: The OSR Light / Apprentice Path

46:10 — 59:43

Reframing the SDR role from day one as a sales apprenticeship. Hire with sales potential in mind, require them to prove themselves on cold calling first, then gradually expose them to the full sales cycle.

  • Phase 1: SDR must first prove they can "mow the grass"—run campaigns, make calls, get consistent results. Don't skip this step or oversell the future role
  • Phase 2: Start sending them to trade shows, conferences, networking events. They learn the market face-to-face in unscripted environments
  • Phase 3: Introduce warmer channels (inbound, referrals, event leads). Always start SDRs on hard channels first—if they start easy, they'll resist outbound later
  • Phase 4: Shadow sales reps, then run qualifying calls, then shadow discovery, then run discovery, then shadow proposals—each step is the "lowest risk next thing"
  • Two successful SDR-to-salesperson transitions completed; those salespeople now help onboard new SDRs
  • Attracts better SDR candidates because they see a career path, not just a cold-calling grind
  • Requires the SDR to be local (for events), unlike the other two strategies which work with remote/offshore SDRs

Case Study 3: Mowing the Grass 2.0 (Multi-Channel Amplification)

59:43 — 69:43

The easiest to implement: take your existing "pretty good" campaign and amplify it. No fundamental overhaul needed—just extension and channel expansion.

  • Traditional campaign: Mailer Thursday, call Monday/Wednesday/Friday = 4 touches, 2 channels, 1 week. Gets ~4-5 appointments/month
  • 2.0 campaign: 30 days, 5 channels (direct mail, phone, email, LinkedIn, text), 12-15 touch points
  • Each touch point references the previous one: script references the mailer, email references the call, LinkedIn connection references the email, etc.
  • SDRs get templates as frameworks but are expected to personalize—not read verbatim. This is what separates human outreach from AI spam
  • LinkedIn voice notes are especially effective right now (human, unautomated)
  • Text messages: use sparingly (1 per campaign, as a last touch). High open rates but high abuse complaints if overused
  • Build the entire workflow in your CRM (HubSpot, Close, Keap, etc.) with automated task sequences
  • Track all outbound activity across channels, not just calls, for true performance visibility

Action Items

  • Audit your SDR hiring criteria — Define the specific traits (coachability, communication, resilience) required for the role and screen for them, even if hiring entry-level
  • Run a data quality check on your prospect list — Sample 50 records and verify contact names and phone numbers. If 30%+ are wrong, invest in list enrichment before your next campaign
  • Build a tangible offer for your SDR to pitch — Create a toolkit, checklist, assessment, or case study that gives the prospect immediate value in exchange for their time
  • Update your cold call script to a permission-based opener — Lead with transparency ("this is a cold call"), ask for 25 seconds, and give the prospect the power to hang up
  • Set up disposition-based CRM fields — Identify your 5-7 most common reasons prospects don't book, create custom fields for each, and start logging every call outcome
  • Write tailored email sequences for each disposition — Create 24 weekly emails per disposition that subtly address the specific objection over 6 months, with a callback task at the end
  • Extend your campaign from 1 week to 30 days — Add LinkedIn, email, and text to your existing direct mail + phone campaign. Build as a CRM workflow with 12-15 connected touch points
  • Establish weekly SDR sales meetings — Review call recordings, share best practices, track numbers on a dashboard, and provide regular coaching feedback
