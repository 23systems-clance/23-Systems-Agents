Product Specification

23WF Platform Productization

Strategy for turning the 23WF autonomous agent platform into a multi-tier product serving developers, founders, and brand new learners — with templates, courses, and a skill ecosystem.
1

Executive Summary

23WF is a full-stack autonomous agent platform with a two-layer architecture (Next.js event handler + Docker agent containers) that already supports chat-driven job creation, cron scheduling, webhook triggers, multi-agent clusters, a plugin skill system, and a branded template engine.

The platform is uniquely positioned because it combines infrastructure (Docker orchestration, GitHub Actions, git-native audit trails) with developer experience (config-driven customization, skill plugins, cluster roles) in a single product. Most competitors only do one.

Core Thesis: A free Skool community becomes the top-of-funnel, giving learners hands-on access to the real platform (10 free runs/month). The community creates content, social proof, and a natural upgrade path into paid tiers. The same platform, packaged four ways, serves learners who want to understand AI agents, community members who want to build together, developers who want to ship with agents, and founders who want to white-label the infrastructure.
What This Spec Covers
  • Skool community strategy — free tier as marketing engine with paid community upsell
  • Four product tiers mapped to the customer journey (Free → Community Pro → Platform Pro → Enterprise)
  • AI course curriculum built on existing templates as progressive modules
  • Template marketplace leveraging the 8 existing config/templates
  • Skill registry enabling community contribution and network effects
  • Revenue model combining community subscriptions with platform billing
  • Four build phases from current state to full multi-tenant SaaS

Target Audiences

Four distinct personas along a single customer journey, each entering at a different point and upgrading as they grow.

The Curious Learner

Who: Students, career changers, non-technical people curious about AI.
Goal: Understand what AI agents can do, build their first one, gain marketable skills.
Pain: Overwhelmed by jargon, don't know where to start, afraid of breaking things.
Entry: Free Skool community, YouTube content, social posts.

The Community Builder

Who: Enthusiasts, freelancers, early adopters who want to learn by doing together.
Goal: More runs, deeper access, peer collaboration, share what they're building.
Pain: Hit the free tier limit, want community feedback and accountability.
Entry: Skool free tier → paid community upgrade.

The Developer

Who: Software engineers, freelancers, technical co-founders.
Goal: Build custom agent workflows, integrate AI into existing systems, ship faster.
Pain: Rolling their own agent infra, unreliable orchestration, no audit trail.
Entry: GitHub, npm package, API docs, Skool community reputation.

The Founder

Who: Agency owners, SaaS founders, operations leaders.
Goal: Ship AI-powered products or automate operations without hiring an ML team.
Pain: Can't build agent infra in-house, need compliance, need white-labeling.
Entry: Sales calls, partner referrals, case studies, community success stories.

Key Insight: The Skool community is the engine. Free members become paying community members, community members become Pro developers, developers become Enterprise founders. Every tier feeds the next. The community creates content, social proof, and accountability that no amount of marketing can replicate.

Skool Community Strategy

The Skool community is the growth engine for the entire product. It replaces a traditional marketing funnel with a community-led flywheel where free members create content, social proof, and word-of-mouth that compounds over time.

Why Skool (Not Discord, Circle, or a Custom Forum)
  • Built-in course hosting — modules, progress tracking, and drip content are native features. No separate LMS needed.
  • Gamification — leaderboards, levels, and points drive engagement without custom development.
  • Free + paid tiers native — Skool handles the paywall, billing, and access control. No Stripe integration needed for the community layer.
  • Discovery — Skool has its own marketplace where communities rank by engagement. Active communities get organic traffic.
  • Simplicity — one feed, one classroom, one calendar. No channel sprawl like Discord.

The Free-to-Paid Conversion Flywheel
  1. Discovery — YouTube content, social posts, and Skool marketplace drive free signups. Each video ends with "Join the free community and get 10 agent runs to try this yourself."
  2. Activation — Free members complete Module 1 using their 10 runs. They see their first agent-generated PR on GitHub. This is the "aha" moment.
  3. Engagement — They post their first build in the community feed. Other members react, comment, share their own builds. Social proof compounds.
  4. Limit Hit — After 10 runs, they want more. The upgrade to Community Pro ($97/mo) gives them 10x runs + the private builders channel. Low friction, high perceived value.
  5. Depth — Community Pro members complete advanced modules, build custom templates, collaborate in the builders channel. They become advocates who attract more free members.
  6. Graduation — Power users who need their own instance, API access, or skill authoring upgrade to Platform Pro ($199/mo). The community already taught them the platform — they're productive immediately.

Learner Experience — The AI Agent Course

The course lives inside the Skool classroom, but every module links to the real 23WF platform. Learners don't watch — they do. Each module gives them a task, they use their agent runs to complete it, and they post their results in the community feed. The course IS the community engagement driver.

Free vs Paid Course Access: Free members can access all 8 modules but are gated by their 10 runs/month. They can read ahead, but they'll hit the run limit around Module 3. Community Pro members ($97/mo) get 100 runs — enough to complete the full course and experiment with remixes.
Course Structure: "Build Your AI Workforce"
Module 1
Your First Agent
Difficulty: Beginner • Time: 30 min • Template: None (raw chat)

Open the chat UI, type a task, watch the agent create a branch, run in Docker, and open a PR. Understand the two-layer architecture by watching it work. No configuration, no setup — just the chat.

  • Concepts: Jobs, branches, PRs, agent lifecycle
  • Outcome: Learner sees their first agent-generated PR on GitHub
Module 2
Configuration & Personality
Difficulty: Beginner • Time: 45 min • File: config/SOUL.md

Edit SOUL.md to change the agent's personality. Understand how config files control behavior without touching code. Introduce secrets and credential management.

  • Concepts: Config-driven customization, LLM prompts, secrets
  • Outcome: Agent responds differently based on personality edits
Module 3
Give Your Agent Eyes
Difficulty: Beginner • Time: 1 hr • Skills: brave-search, youtube-transcript

Activate the brave-search and youtube-transcript skills. Create a job that researches a topic using web search and summarizes a YouTube video. Learn how skills extend agent capability.

  • Concepts: Skills as plugins, activation via symlinks, API keys
  • Outcome: Agent delivers a research report with web + video sources
Module 4
Agent Deliverables
Difficulty: Intermediate • Time: 1 hr • Skill: sop-generator

Use the SOP generator skill to create branded HTML documents. Understand the template system, brand presets, and how agents produce professional output. Remix a template with different branding.

  • Concepts: Template engine, brand system, structured output
  • Outcome: Learner has a branded, print-ready document generated by their agent
Module 5
Scheduled Intelligence
Difficulty: Intermediate • Time: 1 hr • File: config/CRONS.json

Create a cron job that runs daily. Understand the three action types (agent, command, webhook). Build a competitor monitoring cron using the competitor-watch template.

  • Concepts: Cron scheduling, action types, template tokens
  • Outcome: Agent runs autonomously on a schedule with no manual trigger
Module 6
React to Events
Difficulty: Intermediate • Time: 1.5 hr • File: config/TRIGGERS.json

Set up a webhook trigger. Understand template tokens ({{body.field}}). Build a trigger that reacts to external events and dispatches agent jobs. Connect to Slack for notifications.

  • Concepts: Webhooks, triggers, template tokens, event-driven automation
  • Outcome: External event triggers an autonomous agent response
Module 7
Multi-Agent Teams
Difficulty: Advanced • Time: 2 hr • Template: research-team

Deploy the research-team cluster template. Understand roles, dependencies, shared folders, and concurrency control. Modify the template to add a fourth role. Graduate exercise: build a cluster from scratch.

  • Concepts: Clusters, roles, dependencies, orchestration
  • Outcome: Multi-agent team produces a coordinated research deliverable
Module 8
Build Your Own Skill
Difficulty: Advanced • Time: 2 hr • File: SKILL_BUILDING_GUIDE.md

Follow the SKILL_BUILDING_GUIDE to create a custom skill from scratch. Understand SKILL.md frontmatter, scripts, credential setup, and activation. Publish to the skill registry.

  • Concepts: Skill architecture, YAML frontmatter, scripting, registry
  • Outcome: Learner has a working custom skill activated in their instance — ready to upgrade to Pro

Developer Experience

Developers care about three things: how fast they can start, how much control they have, and how debuggable the system is. 23WF already excels at all three.

5-Minute Quick Start
  1. npx 23wf init — scaffolds the project, creates config files, sets up GitHub Actions
  2. Set environment variables (LLM provider key, GitHub PAT)
  3. Open the chat UI, create your first agent job
  4. Watch the PR appear on GitHub with full audit trail
  5. Activate skills by symlinking into skills/active/
Developer-Specific Features
  • Git-native audit trail — every agent action is a commit on a job/* branch. Full reproducibility.
  • Custom LLM routing — per-cron and per-trigger model overrides in JSON config. Use Claude for reasoning, GPT for speed, Gemini for long context.
  • API-first — POST /api/create-job with x-api-key header. Integrate agents into any system.
  • MCP server support — connect to any Model Context Protocol server for extended tool access.
  • Self-hosted option — docker-compose up runs the full stack. No vendor lock-in.
  • Skill SDK — create skills with just a SKILL.md + scripts. No framework code, no build step.
Developer Documentation Structure

The existing 12-document architecture guide (docs/01-architecture.md through docs/12-environment-variables.md) with INDEX.md routing becomes the developer docs for the Pro tier. Add:

  • API Reference — auto-generated from route handlers in Clusters/api/
  • Skill Cookbook — 10 example skills covering common patterns (API wrapper, CLI tool, data pipeline, notification, scraper)
  • Cluster Patterns — reference architectures for fan-out, pipeline, review-gate, and monitor-alert cluster topologies

Template Marketplace

The highest-leverage build because templates serve all three audiences simultaneously: learners browse and follow guided mode, developers fork and customize, founders deploy as vertical solutions.

Current State

8 templates exist in config/templates/ as JSON files with inputs (form fields), cluster definitions (system prompts, roles, dependencies), and shared folder configs. The /templates route is scaffolded in the app.

Target Experience
  1. Browse — Visual gallery of templates with category filters (Research, Content, Development, Operations, Sales), difficulty badges, and preview cards showing roles and workflow diagrams
  2. Preview — Click into a template to see the full workflow: what inputs are required, what roles will be created, how data flows between them, and what the expected output looks like
  3. Deploy — One-click deployment that fills in the input form, creates the cluster, and kicks off the first run. Launchpad users get guided annotations; Pro users get the raw config.
  4. Fork & Remix — Duplicate a template, modify roles/prompts/dependencies, save as a personal template. This is the "learn by doing" mechanism.
  5. Publish — Pro and Enterprise users can submit templates to the community registry. Review process + quality badges.

Competitive Moats

Five structural advantages that are difficult to replicate.

MoatWhat It MeansWhy It's Hard to Copy
Git-Native Audit Trail Every agent action is a git commit on a job/* branch. Full history, diffable, revertible. PRs as the unit of agent output. Requires the two-layer architecture (event handler + Docker agent) to be built from scratch. Retrofitting audit trails onto existing agent platforms is architecturally painful.
Skill Composability Skills are just SKILL.md + scripts. No framework code, no build step, no SDK version to track. Lowest possible barrier to extend. Most platforms require plugin SDKs with version coupling. The markdown-first approach means skills survive framework changes and can be authored by non-engineers.
Config-Over-Code Personality, scheduling, triggers, LLM routing, and cluster orchestration are all JSON/Markdown config. Zero code for 80% of use cases. Requires discipline in the core package to keep logic out of user-facing code. Competitors who start with code-first approaches can't easily add config-driven UX later.
Template-as-Curriculum The same templates that power production workflows also serve as structured learning modules. The course IS the product. Competitors would need both a production platform AND a course platform. 23WF has both because templates are the shared primitive.
Community Flywheel Free Skool community creates content, social proof, templates, and skills that make the platform more valuable. Members teach members. The community IS the marketing. Community moats compound over time. A competitor launching today would need to build both the platform AND grow a community from zero. By the time they catch up on features, the 23WF community has years of shared templates, skills, and institutional knowledge.
Self-Hosted + Managed Duality Same codebase runs as docker-compose up or as a hosted SaaS. Developers who won't touch a SaaS will still use an open-core tool on their infra. Requires the npm package + scaffold architecture. Most SaaS platforms can't easily offer self-hosted mode without forking their codebase.
The Flywheel: Free Skool members try the platform (10 runs) → upgrade to Community Pro ($97/mo) for more runs + community → community members create templates and share builds → their content attracts more free members → power users upgrade to Platform Pro ($199/mo) and build skills → skills attract more developers → founders see the ecosystem and pay Enterprise ($399+/mo) for white-labeled access. Every layer feeds the next, and the community creates value that no competitor can replicate.
